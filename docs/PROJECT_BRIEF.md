# Nautilus: does "sleeping on it" help a learning machine have insights?

*Project brief, written 2026-10-02. This is the founding design document. It
is not a locked protocol, and no experiment has been run yet. The S0
literature check is in `docs/related_work.md`.*

A nautilus shell grows by repeating its own shape at a larger scale: a
complicated form generated by a short, self-similar rule. This project asks
whether an "aha" moment is the discovery of such a short rule, and whether an
offline, sleep-like phase helps a learning machine find one.

---

## 1. Plain-language summary

People often solve problems after "sleeping on it". In a classic experiment,
more than twice as many people discovered a hidden shortcut in a number puzzle
after a night's sleep than after staying awake (Wagner et al., 2004). In a
2025 study, 86% of people who reached deeper sleep during a 20-minute nap had
an "aha" moment, against 56% of those who stayed awake (Löwe et al., 2025).

**Our working theory has three linked parts:**

- **Insight is compression.** An "aha" means finding a *simpler* description
  that connects the dots.
- **Sleep is time for compression without new input.** While offline, the
  brain replays, adds noise and prunes. That can uncover the simpler
  description.
- **Self-similarity (fractal structure) is one kind of simple description.** A
  pattern made of copies of itself can be described very briefly, if you
  notice the self-similarity.

**What we will test, on small machines that run on this mini PC:**

1. Does a sleep-like offline phase produce more insight than the *same amount
   of computation* spent practising awake?
2. Is insight visible as a compression of the machine's internal "thought
   space", and does that compression start *before* the behaviour changes?
3. Does a bias toward self-similar descriptions during sleep help specifically
   when the hidden rule is itself self-similar?

**Why it's worth doing.** Human studies show that sleep helps insight. Simple
neural networks can show human-like "aha" jumps (Löwe et al., 2024). But
nobody seems to have tested whether a sleep-like phase in such a network beats
equal extra practice. The authors of that work explicitly left sleep for future
study.

**Our honest prior** is modest. In both predecessor projects (ACP-CL and
Planaria), simple, well-tuned baselines beat brain-inspired mechanisms. A
clean "sleep is just extra compute" result would still be a useful answer.

## 2. Background (details in `docs/related_work.md`)

- **Humans.** Sleep promotes insight:
  - Wagner et al., 2004, Number Reduction Task;
  - Lacaux et al., 2021, the sleep-onset "sweet spot";
  - Löwe et al., 2025, where deeper N2 naps raised insight from 56% to 86%.

  EEG suggests that deeper sleep acts like **regularisation**, the brain
  scaling down its connection strengths.
- **Networks that have insights.** Löwe et al. (2024) showed that a tiny
  network with L1-regularised attention gates and noisy updates reproduces
  human insight: delayed, sudden, and only in some individuals. Knowledge
  accumulates "silently" before the switch. **Sleep was not tested.**
- **Older computational accounts:**
  - Hélie & Sun (2010), where implicit processing during incubation surfaces
    as explicit insight;
  - Kralik et al. (2016), incubation in robots;
  - a spiking-network model in which time-compressed replay during sleep
    reveals the hidden mirror structure of Wagner's task (Lerner).

  None compares sleep with equal extra waking computation.
- **Insight as compression:**
  - Schmidhuber's compression-progress theory (2008);
  - grokking research, where sudden generalisation coincides with a drop in
    the complexity and intrinsic dimension of a network's representations.
- **Sleep for AI systems:**
  - sleep-like replay against forgetting;
  - DreamCoder's abstraction-and-dream cycle;
  - sleep-time compute for language models;
  - Google/Cornell's 2026 "Sleep" phase for language models.

  These target memory or efficiency, not insight.
- **Fractals and AI:**
  - self-similarity of language;
  - fractal and intrinsic dimension of model representations;
  - fractal compression (Neural Collages);
  - fractal pretraining (FractalDB);
  - fractal generative models.

  No fractal or self-similarity prior has been tested for rule discovery.

## 3. Hypotheses (falsifiable)

**H1, sleep beats equal awake compute.** After the same learning session, an
offline sleep phase leads **more learners** to discover a hidden shortcut, or
leads them to it **sooner**, than an equal amount of extra awake training. The
sleep phase uses no new data: replay, noise and regularisation. The awake
training uses the same stored experiences. "Equal" means the same number of
parameter updates, with the cost of each counted.

**H2, insight is compression, and compression comes first.** In learners that
have the insight, a measurable compression of the internal representation:

- **coincides with** the behavioural switch; and
- **begins before** it, as silent knowledge. Early compression therefore
  *predicts* who will have the insight, and when.

Compression is measured as the intrinsic or correlation ("fractal") dimension
of hidden states, or the description length of the weights. If H1 holds, part
of sleep's benefit runs through this compression.

**H3, self-similar compression for self-similar rules.** A sleep phase biased
toward self-similar descriptions increases insight on hidden rules that are
self-similar, and does **not** on matched rules that are not. The rules that
are not self-similar are the built-in control.

Each hypothesis must be able to fail, and a failed hypothesis is reported.

## 4. Experimental design (for the first protocol)

### Tasks: a ladder, cheapest first

| Rung | Task | Hidden shortcut | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Löwe's perceptual task**, symbolic version: two inputs, motion and colour. Colour becomes predictive without warning. | Switching to the easy colour cue | Replicate published insight statistics; minimal test of H1 |
| 2 | **Number Reduction Task** (Wagner et al., 2004): chained digit rules with a hidden mirror symmetry | The 2nd response already equals the final answer | H1 and H2 in a small network with a real "thought space" |
| 3 | **Self-similar hidden rules**, e.g. sequences generated by substitution rules (fractal sequences), alongside matched rules that are not self-similar | Predicting from the generating rule instead of memorising | H3 |

A rung is climbed only if the previous one gives a clean result.

### Arms (conditions after the first learning session)

| Arm | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Baseline snapshot** | Nothing: the learner is measured as it stands at the end of session 1 | The floor. Free to obtain. |
| **Awake** | Extra training on the same stored experiences, with the same compute budget as sleep. **Its regularisation and noise are tuned too.** | The strongest fair competitor: the same ingredients, used continuously while awake |
| **Sleep** | An offline phase with no new data: replay of stored experiences, plus noise, plus regularisation (scaling connections down) | The hypothesis |
| **Sleep ablations** | Replay only; noise only; scaling-down only | Which ingredient matters? |
| *(Optional)* **Awake + fresh data** | Extra training on new experiences | Reference: what more experience buys |

**Why there is no "quiet rest" arm** (owner's point, 2026-10-02). A frozen
network that is not computing does not change. So "rest" equals the baseline
snapshot, which we measure anyway. In humans, a rest group controls for the
passage of time and for spontaneous brain activity. An AI has neither, unless
we build that activity in, and a built-in version would be a form of sleep.

**Fairness rules:**

- **Equal compute.** Equal update steps and equal counted FLOPs for awake and
  sleep. Wall time is reported.
- **Equal tuning budgets.** The awake arm may use any continuous
  regularisation or noise schedule, so sleep must beat the best continuous
  alternative, not a weak one. This lesson comes from Planaria, where Stable
  SGD won.

### Measures

- **Insight (behavioural):**
  - whether a learner switches to the shortcut;
  - when it switches;
  - how abruptly, via the slope of a sigmoid fitted at the switch point, as in
    Löwe et al.;
  - the share of learners that switch at all.

  For the Number Reduction Task, insight means answering after the 2nd step
  with accuracy maintained.
- **Compression (internal):**
  - intrinsic dimension (TwoNN) and correlation dimension of hidden-state
    clouds;
  - effective rank;
  - gate sparsity;
  - weight description length.
- **Lead time:** the change point in compression, minus the behavioural switch
  point.
- **Costs:** update steps, FLOPs, wall time.

### Statistics

- Many learners per condition: they are cheap, so hundreds.
- Separate development and test seeds.
- At most 5 pre-declared criteria per protocol, with per-seed consistency
  rules and paired bootstrap intervals.

## 6. Method rules (carried over from ACP-CL and Planaria)

1. **Protocol before data.** Declare development knobs, seeds, at most 5
   criteria and validity checks, then lock (hashes plus a lock commit) before
   any test-seed run.
2. **Calibrate against a ceiling and a floor.**
   - Ceiling: a learner that is told the shortcut, i.e. its gate is open from
     the start.
   - Floor: the baseline snapshot.
3. **Strongest fair competitor:** the tuned awake arm (§4).
4. **Validity checks:**
   - headroom (insight must be neither near 0% nor near 100% in the floor);
   - competence (learners must learn the basic task);
   - equal compute verified, not assumed.
5. **Report negatives at full detail.** Never tune on test seeds or relax
   thresholds after seeing data.
6. **Integrity:**
   - deterministic seeds and one thread per worker;
   - a replicate pass on a subset of runs. Compare *results*, not file paths:
     that was the Planaria D1 lesson.
   - Hash the result files.
7. **Fractals carry a credibility risk.** "Fractals + intelligence" attracts
   pseudo-science. Use fractal ideas only in one precise, testable role at a
   time, each with a non-fractal control.
8. **Run lean.** CPU-scale, small scripts, few or no subagents. Cloud compute
   only after a GO-level result.

---

## 7. Risks

- **Sleep may simply equal extra compute.** That is the most likely negative,
  and still an answer.
- **Insight may be too rare or too common to measure.** The headroom check
  catches this; tune the task difficulty on development seeds.
- **Compression measures are noisy** in small networks. Use several measures
  and pre-declare a primary one.
- **The tiny rung-1 model** (four parameters) has no meaningful internal space.
  H2 and H3 need rungs 2–3.
